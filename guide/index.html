User guide

Kenanga Signal Board

A daily digest of Kenanga Investment Bank research, with every figure checked against the source report. This page explains how to read it — and, more importantly, how not to misread it.

Reading a row in thirty seconds

  1. Pick a date from the dropdown at the top. Each date is one trading day's published bundle.
  2. Check the verification strip underneath. It tells you how many reports were verified against their source PDFs, and how many carry a data-quality flag.
  3. Section 1 is the market backdrop from that morning's Kenanga Today digest — index close, fund flows, corporate news, macro.
  4. Section 2 is every report Kenanga published that day. Filter by report type using the dropdown.
  5. Click any row to open the detail panel: rating, target price, five takeaways, the quoted line the summary rests on, and a link to the original PDF.

The two signal columns

Both columns answer one narrow question: did an analyst declare conviction in their own words? Not "is this a good stock" — that is what the Rating and Target price columns are for.

Kenanga Signal

POSITIVE

Kenanga called the stock a Top Pick, one of its sector picks, or the most attractive among peers. The exact sentence is quoted on the row so you can check it yourself.

Analyst Signal

POSITIVE

The same test applied to a named non-Kenanga house — CIMB Securities, RHB, AmInvestment and others, as reported by The Edge Malaysia. Every call names the house and carries its date.

Nothing is inferred. A house can rate a stock BUY with 30% implied upside and the row still reads None stated, because it never said the stock was its pick.

“None stated” is not a negative view

This is the one thing worth getting right. None stated is a fact about the report, not a judgement on the stock. It means the analyst did not use pick language in that note — nothing more.

Plenty of Outperform-rated stocks with rising targets read None stated. For the house's actual stance, read the Rating and Target price columns. The signal columns only tell you where someone went further and said so explicitly.

Marks you'll see on a row

MarkMeaningWhat to do
⚑ Data-quality flag. Something in this row needed judgement — a figure inferred rather than printed, a share price outside its 52-week range, a misdated source file. Open the row. The flag explains itself in full.
⇆ House divergence. A named house rates this stock the opposite way to Kenanga. Read it. Disagreement between two houses that both did the work is where the decision actually sits.

Use the Stated conviction filter to see only rows where either signal is Positive.

What this board is not

  • Not investment advice. Ratings and target prices are Kenanga's. The five takeaways under each row are AI-written interpretation, labelled as such, with every claim traceable to a quoted line in the cited report.
  • Not full analyst coverage. Malaysian broker research is client-only. The Analyst Signal draws on what The Edge Malaysia and the press report publicly — nine houses searched, publicly retrievable calls found for five. “None stated” means no conviction found in accessible sources, never that no house holds a view.
  • Not always same-day. Most Analyst Signal calls are standing sector picks published weeks or months before the Kenanga report beside them. Each row's summary says how old the call is.
  • Not a consensus average. Anonymous aggregates were deliberately removed. Where no second house covers a stock, the board says so rather than filling the gap — Kenanga states it has sole coverage of TGUAN and BPPLAS, for instance.
  • Market data is not Kenanga's. 52-week ranges and dividend yields come from KLSE market data and are live as at fetch, not as at the report date. That timing gap is what raises most ⚑ flags.

How it stays current

The board updates automatically Tuesday to Saturday at midnight Malaysia time, each run adding the previous trading day. Kenanga publishes in the morning, so a Tuesday midnight run collects Monday's reports.

Before anything publishes, ten automated checks run against the new data — schema, source URLs, duplicates, cross-date reconciliation, and a check that every quoted conviction sentence is a genuine quotation. If any blocking check fails, nothing publishes and the previous day's board stays live. The verification strip on each date shows what passed.

A missing day means Kenanga published no bundle, or a check failed. It never means a day was invented to fill the gap.
